HOW THIS ACTUALLY WORKS

Can we win the whole £1,700 jackpot?
YES — completely. Prizes are fixed per place: 1st takes the full £1,700, 2nd £1,200, 3rd £900, 4th £600, 5th £387. Nothing is ever split between entries. If two entries finish level on points, the tiebreaker (our predicted minute of the first goal in the final — 24, 35 and 12) decides who takes the higher prize outright. Entry date is irrelevant.
Then what's the problem with people having the same picks as us?
It's about position on the points table, not money. In our perfect tournament, nine entries with near-identical teams score almost exactly what we score, at the same moment. Five paid places. Whether we sit above or below them comes down to the one or two picks that differ — our Saudi Arabia against Ben Corry's Panama. Our points never shrink; our rank is what's contested.
How do our teams score points?
Win = opponent's rating ×2 in groups, ×4 in knockouts (beat Spain in a knockout = 400). Draw (groups only) = rating ×1 — Austria drawing Argentina = 80. Every goal = 3 (6 in knockouts), clean sheet = 5 (10), cards cost 1–3. The joker's points all count double.
Why does it matter how many people picked our joker?
The joker is roughly half a ticket's score. If France win, all 229 France-joker entries rocket together — nobody gains. If Spain win, only 178 surge, and our T2 is among them with a one-of-a-kind supporting cast. Pools are won by being right AND rare.
Why is T2 our "best" ticket when T1 scores more?
T1 is the 2nd-highest-scoring design in the pool — but it has a twin and eight near-twins, so its glory is shared. T2 is the only entry of its kind among all 1,080. Quality earns points; scarcity earns prizes.
What does "gap to the money" mean?
The points between our best ticket and whoever currently sits 5th — the last paid place (£387). That's the line we hunt all tournament.
What are the percentages?
Estimates from 10,000 simulated tournaments scored with the real rules and raced against the real 1,080 entries. Weather forecasts, not promises.
